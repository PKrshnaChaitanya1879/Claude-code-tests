# Design bible: what the book says

Extracted from the Lewis Mercier translation (`reference/book/twenty_thousand_leagues_mercier.txt`). Mercier's chapter numbers differ from the French edition (he merges chapters), so citations give **Mercier part and chapter** first, with the French chapter in brackets where it differs. Quotes are verbatim from Mercier.

**Units.** Verne wrote in metres. Mercier converted carelessly: his "yards" for the interior are Verne's metres, and his 232 ft × 26 ft is Verne's 70 m × 8 m. **The game uses Verne's metres.**

Plates referenced (`imNNN`) are in `reference/illustrations/CATALOGUE.md`.

### The platform and the cages (I.XIII "The Black River" [Fr. XIV])

> "Toward the middle of the platform the long-boat, half buried in the hull of the vessel, formed a slight excrescence. Fore and aft rose two cages of medium height with inclined sides, and partly closed by thick lenticular glasses; one destined for the steersman who directed the Nautilus, the other containing a brilliant lantern to give light on the road."

- The platform is a flat deck on the hull top, reached by "the central staircase … the iron steps" (I.XIII).
- The rail can be electrified: "a metallic cable charged with electricity" (I.XXI).
- Nemo leans on "the cage of the watch-light" (I.XVII).
- Plates im027 / im050 show the cage as a low box with sloping sides and large round lenticular ports; ill-004 shows a round hatch on the platform.

### The boat (I.XI "All by Electricity" [Fr. XII])

> "This boat is attached to the upper part of the hull of the Nautilus, and occupies a cavity made for it. It is decked, quite water-tight, and held together by solid bolts."

- An iron ladder in a well amidships leads up to a man-hole that matches one in the boat.

### The saloon windows (I.XIII)

> "Suddenly light broke at each side of the saloon, through two oblong openings … Two crystal plates separated us from the sea … strong bands of copper bound them."
>
> "The obscurity of the saloon showed to advantage the brightness outside, and we looked out as if this pure crystal had been the glass of an immense aquarium."

- **Two oblong windows, one on each side** of the saloon, banded in copper, behind sliding iron panels ("A sliding noise was heard: one would have said that panels were working at the sides").
- The saloon goes dark and the sea is lit by the Nautilus's own light: "It was no longer luminous water, but liquid light."
- Plate im029 shows an arched frame with slender columns.

### Layout of the forward part (I.XI [Fr. XII])

> "the dining-room, five yards long, separated from the library by a water-tight partition; the library, five yards long; the large drawing-room, ten yards long, separated from the Captain's room by a second water-tight partition; the said room, five yards in length; mine, two and a half yards; and, lastly a reservoir of air, seven and a half yards, that extended to the bows. - The robing-room ("arsenal and wardrobe") and the flooding cell are aft "near the machinery-room" (I.XIV / I.XV).
- Engine room power: "I use Bunsen's contrivances, not Ruhmkorff's … Bunsen's are fewer in number, but strong and large"; the current "works, by electro-magnets of great size, on a system of levers and cog-wheels" on the screw shaft. Plate im024 shows riveted cylinders, a toothed flywheel and grating.

### Dining room (I.IX/X)

> "a dining-room, decorated and furnished in severe taste. High oaken sideboards, inlaid with ebony, stood at the two extremities of the room, and upon their shelves glittered china, porcelain, and glass of inestimable value. The plate on the table sparkled in the rays which the luminous ceiling shed around, while the light was tempered and softened by exquisite paintings."

- Tableware: each piece engraved with an N and the motto **MOBILIS IN MOBILI** (I.VIII).
- Plate: im020.

### Library (I.X)

> "High pieces of furniture, of black violet ebony inlaid with brass, supported upon their wide shelves a great number of books uniformly bound. They followed the shape of the room, terminating at the lower part in huge divans, covered with brown leather, which were curved … Light movable desks, made to slide in and out at will … In the centre stood an immense table, covered with pamphlets … The electric light flooded everything; it was shed from four unpolished globes half sunk in the volutes of the ceiling."

- "Twelve thousand" volumes. No political economy. Also a smoking-room: a "little brazier, which was supported upon an elegant bronze stem."
- Plate: im021 (curved shelves, continuous divan, ceiling globes).

### Saloon (I.X)

> "It was a vast four-sided room, thirty feet long, eighteen wide, and fifteen high. A luminous ceiling, decorated with light arabesques, shed a soft clear light over all the marvels accumulated in this museum."

Verne's figures are **10 m × 6 m × 5 m**.

- **Paintings:** "Thirty first-rate pictures, uniformly framed, separated by bright drapery … walls, which were hung with tapestry of severe design."
  - Old masters: a Madonna of Raphael, a Virgin of Leonardo, a nymph of Correggio, a woman of Titian, an Adoration of Veronese, an Assumption of Murillo, a portrait of Holbein, a monk of Velázquez, a martyr of Ribera, a fair of Rubens, two Flemish landscapes of Teniers, three genre pictures of Gerard Dou, Metsu and Paulus Potter, two of Géricault and Prud'hon, sea-pieces of Backhuysen and Vernet.
  - Moderns: Delacroix, Ingres, Decamps, Troyon, Meissonier, Daubigny.
  - The Met CC0 set in `reference/assets/paint/` covers these where possible.
- **Statues:** "some admirable statues in marble and bronze, after the finest antique models, stood upon pedestals in the corners."
- **Organ:** "a large model piano-organ which occupied one of the panels." Scores of Weber, Rossini, Mozart, Beethoven, Haydn, Meyerbeer, Hérold, Wagner, Auber, Gounod lie on it.
  - Nemo plays only the black keys, "which gave his melodies an essentially Scotch character" (I.XXII), i.e. pentatonic. Use this for the organ cues.
- **Table:** "a costly mosaic table."
- **Cases:** "Under elegant glass cases, fixed by copper rivets, were classed and labelled the most precious productions of the sea."
  - Zoophytes: gorgones "arranged like a fan", Syrian sponges, isis, pennatules, virgularia, madrepores, corals.
  - Echinoderms: sea-stars, comatules, echini, holothuri.
  - Molluscs: royal hammer-fish, imperial spondyle, aspergillum, trochi.
  - Pearls "in separate compartments … pink … green … yellow, blue and black", some "larger than a pigeon's egg."
- **Fountain:** a giant tridacna shell basin with a jet. It is not in this chapter of Mercier, but it is in plate im022 and in the French text (the bénitier fountain). Use the plate.
- **Instruments:** duplicates of Nemo's instruments hang on the saloon walls ("I see suspended on the walls of this room instruments").
- **Windows:** see section 1.

### Nemo's room (I.X, I.XI)

> "a severe, almost a monkish, aspect. A small iron bedstead, a table, some articles for the toilet; the whole lighted by a skylight. No comforts, the strictest necessaries only."

The instrument wall (I.XI), every instrument named:

- thermometer
- barometer
- hygrometer
- storm-glass
- compass
- sextant
- chronometers
- glasses for day and night
- **manometer**: "by communication with the water, whose external pressure it indicates, it gives our depth at the same time"
- **electric clock**: "divided … into twenty-four hours, like the Italian clocks"
- **speed dial**: "An electric thread puts it in communication with the screw, and the needle indicates the real speed"

Plate im023 / ill-001 shows the dials on the wall, the iron bed with scroll ends and the wash-stand.

### Aronnax's room (I.X)

> "an elegant room, with a bed, dressing-table, and several other pieces of furniture." It is 2.5 m long and adjoins Nemo's.

### Robing-room and flooding cell (I.XV "A Walk on the Bottom of the Sea" [Fr. XVI])

- "This cell was, to speak correctly, the arsenal and wardrobe of the Nautilus. A dozen diving apparatuses hung from the partition."
- Two crewmen help them dress.
- The flooding cell is "a little room contiguous to the wardrobe-room":

> "I heard a water-tight door, furnished with stopper-plates, close upon us, and we were wrapped in profound darkness. After some minutes, a loud hissing was heard. I felt the cold mount from my feet to my chest. Evidently from some part of the vessel they had, by means of a tap, given entrance to the water … A second door cut in the side of the Nautilus then opened. We saw a faint light."

- Return (I.XVI): "The outside door had been left open, and Captain Nemo closed it as soon as we had entered the first cell. He then pressed a knob. I heard the pumps working … I felt the water sinking from around me … The inside door then opened, and we entered the vestry."
- **Game sequence:**
  1. Close the inner door (stopper-plates).
  2. Hold the tap: hiss, cold rising, gauge climbing.
  3. Open the outer side door to a faint light.
  4. On return: close the outer door, press the pump knob, water drains, inner door.

## 3. The diving dress (I.XIV "A Note of Invitation" [Fr. XV], I.XV [Fr. XVI])

| Part | Book |
|---|---|
| Suit | "heavy and impervious clothes, made of india-rubber without seam … both supple and resisting. This suit formed trousers and waistcoat." |
| Boots | "thick boots, weighted with heavy leaden soles" |
| Chest | "The texture of the waistcoat was held together by bands of copper, which crossed the chest" |
| Gloves | "the sleeves ended in gloves, which in no way restrained the movement of the hands" |
| Collar | "a copper collar upon which was screwed the metal helmet" |
| Helmet | "Three holes, protected by thick glass, allowed us to see in all directions, by simply turning our head" |
| Rouquayrol | "a reservoir of thick iron plates, in which I store the air under a pressure of fifty atmospheres … fixed on the back by means of braces, like a soldier's knapsack. Its upper part forms a box in which the air is kept by means of a bellows"; two india-rubber pipes, "the inspirator and the expirator", open into the copper ball; "nine or ten hours" of air |
| Ruhmkorff lamp | "one is carried on the back, the other is fastened to the waist … a Bunsen pile … worked … with sodium … a particularly made lantern. In this lantern is a spiral glass which contains a small quantity of carbonic gas … giving out a white and continuous light"; switched on "by turning a screw"; four lanterns light "a circle of thirty-six yards" |
| Air gun | "the butt end of which, made of steel, hollow in the centre … served as a reservoir for compressed air … A box of projectiles … contained about twenty of these electric balls"; the balls are "little cases of glass … covered with a case of steel, and weighted with a pellet of lead; they are real Leyden bottles" |
| Mission II | no lamps ("They will be useless"); "an iron-pointed stick" |

- **Suit-up order (Mission I):**
  1. Suit and boots.
  2. Gun inspected.
  3. Helmet screwed onto the collar; the Rouquayrol starts.
  4. Ruhmkorff lamp "hanging from my belt", gun in hand.
- **Mission II order:** reservoirs on the back, no lamps, the helmet, then "an iron-pointed stick into my hand."
- **Weight:** aboard, "glued to the deck by my leaden soles, it was impossible for me to take a step." Underwater, "I no longer felt the weight of my clothing … my head rattled like an almond in its shell."
- **Movement design:** suited movement in air is nearly impossible (the crew push you into the cell). Underwater it is slow and buoyant-heavy.
- **Communication:** none ("impossible to speak"). Nemo beckons by signs; Aronnax puts his "great copper head to Conseil's."
- **Plates:** im033 / ill-003 (dress, helmet with three round ports, reservoir, lamp at the belt), im036, im053.

## 4. Mission I

### The walk (I.XV [Fr. XVI]): Crespo Island, 17 November, 10 a.m.

1. **Start:** "the Nautilus is stranded in five fathoms" (~9 m). "Our feet trod the bottom of the sea."
2. **Light:**
   - "The light, which lit the soil thirty feet below the surface … astonished me by its power … I clearly distinguished objects at a distance of a hundred and fifty yards. Beyond that the tints darkened into fine gradations of ultramarine, and faded into vague obscurity."
   - **Visibility ~150 m, fading to ultramarine.** This is not cyan soup.
3. **Sand:** "fine, even sand, not wrinkled … This dazzling carpet, really a reflector, repelled the rays of the sun with wonderful intensity." It is "sown with the impalpable dust of shells."
4. **Prism colours (the vibrancy brief):**
   - "the rays of the sun struck the surface of the waves at rather an oblique angle, and at the touch of their light, decomposed by refraction as through a prism, flowers, rocks, plants, shells, and polypi were shaded at the edges by the seven solar colours … a perfect kaleidoscope of green, yellow, orange, violet, indigo, and blue."
   - **Spectral fringing on edges in the shallows is canon.** Do it as a subtle chromatic rim.
5. **The garden:** isis, tuft-coral, prickly fungi, anemones, porphitae "decked with their collarettes of blue tentacles", sea-stars, asterophytons "like fine lace", molluscs underfoot.
6. **Overhead:** "physalides leaving their tentacles to float in their train, medusæ whose umbrellas of opal or rose-pink, escalloped with a band of blue, sheltered us from the rays of the sun."
7. **Ooze, then a sea-weed prairie:** "soft to the feet." Overhead, "long ribbons of fucus floating." Green plants keep nearer the top, red ones deeper, brown and black deepest.
8. **Noon:** the sun is vertical, "The magical colours disappeared by degrees, and the shades of emerald and sapphire were effaced."
   - Sound: "the slightest noise was transmitted with a quickness to which the ear is unaccustomed."
   - Depth: "a hundred and five yards and twenty inches, undergoing a pressure of six atmospheres." (Mercier's figures are inconsistent here: 105 yards would be ~10 atmospheres, not six. The game compresses depth anyway: **the walk descends from ~9 m to ~30 m by the forest edge**, with the depth mapping in `uw` stretching the descent.)
   - "a reddish twilight" at the forest edge.

### The forest (I.XVI [Fr. XVII])

- **Perpendicularity:** "all stretched up to the surface of the ocean. Not a filament, not a ribbon … but kept as straight as a rod of iron … Truly it was the region of perpendicularity!" Forest plants are **rigidly vertical**, with almost no sway.
- **Colours:** "pink, carmine, green, olive, fawn, and brown."
- **Species:** pavonari "spread like a fan", scarlet ceramies, laminaries, nereocysti "fifteen feet", acetabuli whose stems widen upward.
- **Undergrowth:** "real bushes of living flowers, hedges of zoophytes": zebra-meandrines, yellow caryophylliae.
- **Fish:** "fish-flies flew from branch to branch like a swarm of humming-birds"; yellow lepisacanthi, dactylopteri and monocentrides "rose at our feet like a flight of snipes."
- **Halt:** "an arbour of alariæ, the long thin blades of which stood up like arrows." They sleep.
- **Sea-spider:** "a monstrous sea-spider, about thirty-eight inches high … ready to spring"; Nemo knocks it over with the gun butt.
- **The narrow valley, lamps on:**
  - "a narrow valley, between high perpendicular walls, situated about seventy-five fathoms deep."
  - "At ten paces not an object was visible … Captain Nemo had just put his electric apparatus into use."
  - "the sea, lit by our four lanterns, was illuminated for a circle of thirty-six yards." Plate im036.
- **The end of Nemo's domain:** "A wall of superb rocks … an enormous, steep granite shore, forming dark grottos … Here ended Captain Nemo's domains."
- **Return:**
  - "the sun being low on the horizon, the refraction edged the different objects with a spectral ring."
  - At 10 m: "a shoal of little fishes of all kinds, more numerous than the birds of the air."
  - Nemo shoots a **sea-otter**: "five feet long … chestnut-brown above and silvery underneath … rounded head … short ears … white whiskers … webbed feet … tufted tail."
  - Near the surface, total internal reflection: "I then saw our image clearly reflected, drawn inversely … they walked with their heads downward." **Canon for our TIR mirror.**
  - "the shadows of large birds passing above our heads." An albatross is shot.
  - "the lantern of the Nautilus … for a half mile, broke the darkness."
  - **Sharks:** "two formidable sharks … tintoreas … with enormous tails and a dull glassy stare, the phosphorescent matter ejected from holes pierced around the muzzle … silver bellies." Nemo pushes them flat under algae. A stealth beat.

### The coral kingdom and the burial (I.XXIII "The Coral Kingdom" [Fr. XXIV])

- **Prologue:**
  - A wounded crewman: "a man about forty years of age, with a resolute expression … His head, swathed in bandages." Plate im052.
  - He dies in the night. Aronnax hears "distant sighs like the notes of a funeral psalm."
- **Start:** "At half-past eight we were equipped … accompanied by Captain Nemo, who was followed by a dozen of the crew, we set foot, at a depth of about thirty feet."
- **Coral:**
  - "A slight declivity ended in an uneven bottom, at fifteen fathoms depth … no fine sand, no submarine prairies, no sea-forest … It was the coral kingdom."
  - Coral polyps retract when touched: "The white petals re-entered their red cases, the flowers faded as I looked, and the bush changed into a block of stony knobs." **An interaction:** reach toward coral and the polyps close.
  - Red coral, "Flower of Blood" and "Froth of Blood"; pink coral; "macciota."
- **The gallery:**
  - "Real petrified thickets, long joists of fantastic architecture … Captain Nemo placed himself under a dark gallery, where by a slight declivity we reached a depth of a hundred yards."
  - "The light from our lamps produced sometimes magical effects, following the rough outlines of the natural arches and pendants disposed like lustres, that were tipped with points of fire." Lamps light coral pendants like chandeliers.
- **The coral tree forest:** "an immense forest of large mineral vegetations, enormous petrified trees, united by garlands of elegant sea-bindweed … We passed freely under their high branches." Depth "about three hundred yards" (**the game compresses this: deep, below sunlight, lit only by lamps**).
- **The glade:**
  - "the centre of a vast glade surrounded by the lofty foliage of the submarine forest."
  - "Our lamps threw over this place a sort of clear twilight that singularly elongated the shadows on the ground. At the end of the glade the darkness increased, and was only relieved by little sparks reflected by the points of coral."
- **Graves:** "slight excrescences encrusted with limy deposits, and disposed with a regularity that betrayed the hand of man."
- **Cross:** "on a pedestal of rocks roughly piled up, stood a cross of coral that extended its long arms that one might have thought were made of petrified blood."
- **Ceremony, in order:**
  1. The men form "a semi-circle round their chief"; "four of them carried on their shoulders an object of an oblong shape."
  2. "Upon a sign from Captain Nemo one of the men advanced; and at some feet from the cross he began to dig a hole with a pickaxe that he took from his belt."
  3. "the fish fled on all sides … I heard the strokes of the pickaxe, which sparkled when it hit upon some flint."
  4. "the bearers approached; the body, enveloped in a tissue of white linen, was lowered into the damp grave."
  5. "Captain Nemo, with his arms crossed on his breast, and all the friends of him who had loved them, knelt in prayer."
  6. "The grave was then filled in with the rubbish taken from the ground, which formed a slight mound."
  7. "Captain Nemo and his men rose; then, approaching the grave, they knelt again, and all extended their hands in sign of a last adieu."
  8. "the funeral procession returned … under the arches of the forest … At last the light of the ship appeared, and its luminous track guided us."
- **Epilogue on the platform:** "We dug the grave, and the polypi undertake to seal our dead for eternity … Your dead sleep quietly, at least, Captain, out of the reach of sharks." / "Yes, sir, of sharks and men."
- **Plate:** im053 / ill-005. Nemo stands with a lamp by the cross, the others kneel, a pickaxe leans by them, and a coral canopy hangs above.

## 5. Mission II: a vanished continent (II.IX)

- **Setup:**
  - "That night, about eleven o'clock … 'You have hitherto only visited the submarine depths by daylight … Would it suit you to see them in the darkness of the night?'"
  - "I warn you, the way will be tiring. We shall have far to walk, and must climb a mountain. The roads are not well kept."
  - Only Nemo and Aronnax go.
- **No lamps:** "no electric lamps were prepared … 'They will be useless,' he replied." Aronnax is given "an iron-pointed stick."
- **Depth:** "we set foot on the bottom of the Atlantic at a depth of 150 fathoms. Midnight was near." (Fr. 300 m.)
- **The glow:** "a reddish spot, a sort of large light shining brilliantly about two miles from the Nautilus … it did light our way, vaguely."
- **Rain:**
  - "I heard a kind of pattering above my head. The noise redoubling, sometimes producing a continual shower … It was rain falling violently, and crisping the surface of the waves."
  - He laughs at the idea of getting wet. **Rain audio from below is canon**, even at 300 m in Verne's fiction. Keep it audible but filtered.
- **The stony plain:**
  - "Medusae, microscopic crustacea, and pennatules lit it slightly with their phosphorescent gleam … stone covered with millions of zoophytes and masses of sea weed. My feet often slipped upon this sticky carpet of sea weed."
  - Looking back, "the whitish lantern of the Nautilus beginning to pale in the distance."
- **The mountain:**
  - "The white glimmer came in rays from the summit of a mountain about 800 feet high … The source of this inexplicable light was a fire on the opposite side of the mountain."
  - Nemo is "outlined in black on the luminous horizon." **The INSIDE silhouette shot, straight from the book.**
- **The petrified copse:**
  - "a copse of dead trees, without leaves, without sap, trees petrified by the action of the water and here and there overtopped by gigantic pines … whose branches, like fine black paper cuttings, showed distinctly on the watery ceiling."
  - "their under parts dark and wild, the upper coloured with red tints." **Lighting rule:** lit from above and behind by the red glow; undersides black.
  - "We climbed rocks which fell directly after with gigantic bounds and the low growling of an avalanche." Rockfall audio.
  - Fishes "flew from branch to branch." Sea bind-weed hangs between trees.
- **Traversal:**
  - "Now I jumped a crevice, the depth of which would have made me hesitate had it been among the glaciers … now I ventured on the unsteady trunk of a tree thrown across from one abyss to the other."
  - "monumental rocks, leaning on their regularly-cut bases, seemed to defy all laws of equilibrium."
- **Crustaceans:**
  - "Two hours after quitting the Nautilus we had crossed the line of trees, and a hundred feet above our heads rose the top of the mountain."
  - "enormous antennae blocking my road, or some frightful claw closing with a noise in the shadow of some cavity. Millions of luminous spots shone brightly in the midst of the darkness. They were the eyes of giant crustacea crouched in their holes; giant lobsters setting themselves up like halberdiers, and moving their claws with the clicking sound of pincers; titanic crabs, pointed like a gun on its carriage; and frightful-looking poulps, interweaving their tentacles like a living nest of serpents." Plate im078.
- **First platform ruins:** "vast heaps of stone, amongst which might be traced the vague and shadowy forms of castles and temples, clothed with a world of blossoming zoophytes … sea-weed and fucus threw a thick vegetable mantle … like cromlechs of prehistoric times." Nemo points: "Come, come along; come higher!"
- **Summit:** "for a circle of ten yards commanded the whole mass of rock." The far side drops twice the height.
- **The volcano:**
  - "At fifty feet above the peak, in the midst of a rain of stones and scoriae, a large crater was vomiting forth torrents of lava which fell in a cascade of fire into the bosom of the liquid mass … this volcano lit the lower plain like an immense torch."
  - "**lava, but no flames** … streams of lava … can attain a white heat … and turn it to vapour by contact." Steam where lava meets water; no fire.
- **The city:** "its roofs open to the sky, its temples fallen, its arches dislocated, its columns lying on the ground … the massive character of Tuscan architecture. Further on, some remains of a gigantic aqueduct; here the high base of an Acropolis, with the floating outline of a Parthenon; there traces of a quay … long lines of sunken walls and broad, deserted streets—a perfect Pompeii escaped beneath the waters."
- **The word:** "picking up a piece of chalk-stone, advanced to a rock of black basalt, and traced the one word: ATLANTIS."
- **The hour:**

## 7. Characters

- **Captain Nemo** (I.VII [Fr. VIII]):
  - Build: "He was tall, had a large forehead, straight nose, a clearly cut mouth, beautiful teeth, with fine taper hands."
  - Eyes: "his black eyes looked around with cold assurance … skin, rather pale"; "eyes, rather far from each other."
  - Age: "thirty-five or fifty years of age."
  - Clothing: "caps made from the fur of the sea otter, and shod with sea boots of seal's skin … clothes of a particular texture."
  - Plates im016 / im023: a short beard, white shirt with cravat, sash, light trousers, boots.
- **Pierre Aronnax:** a professor at the Paris Museum, a doctor too (I.XXIII). Plates show him slighter, dark-haired, in a white shirt.
- **Conseil:** "a true, devoted Flemish boy … phlegmatic by nature, regular from principle … very quick with his hands." A classifier.
- **Ned Land:** "a Canadian … about forty years of age; he was a tall man (more than six feet high), strongly built, grave and taciturn, occasionally violent." The harpooner. Plate im008: knit jersey, rolled sleeves, beard, harpoon. **He refuses the diving dress in Mission I.**
- **Crew:**
  - The second, Nemo's companion: "short, very muscular, broad-shouldered, with robust limbs, strong head, an abundance of black hair, thick moustache … the vivacity which characterises the population of Southern France."
  - The dead man: "about forty years of age, with a resolute expression of countenance, a true type of an Anglo-Saxon."
  - Mission I's companion is "a sort of Hercules."
  - The crew speak "a sonorous, harmonious, and flexible dialect."
