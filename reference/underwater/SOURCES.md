## Bamboo coral (Isis hippuris): I · The sand plain
> various kinds of isis  
Shape: Stiff fans of short knobbly branches, growing in one plane; jointed stems.  
Colour: Mustard to olive in sun, brown in shade.  
Scale: Knee to waist high.

## Cushion coral (Cladocora caespitosa): I · The sand plain
> clusters of pure tuft-coral  
Shape: A dome of packed tubes, each with a polyp of fine tentacles.  
Colour: Olive-brown; polyps paler, translucent.  
Scale: Fist to football size.

## Mushroom coral (Fungia): I · The sand plain
> prickly fungi  
Shape: Free-lying oval discs, ribbed from the centre like a mushroom cap upside down.  
Colour: Violet, rose and brown; bright where the sun lands.  
Scale: Palm to plate size, lying loose on sand.

## Giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera): I · The Crespo forest
> This forest was composed of large tree-plants ... their stems rose perpendicularly  
Shape: Tall stipes straight up from a holdfast, leaf-blades on gas bladders all the way up; a canopy at the surface.  
Colour: Gold-olive with the sun behind it; dark silhouettes against the shafts.  
Scale: 10–30 m tall: the forest canopy, far over the diver.

## Precious red coral (Corallium rubrum): The coral kingdom
> The white petals re-entered their red cases ... "Flower of Blood"  
Shape: Small stiff branched bushes hanging from cave roofs and overhangs; white eight-tentacled polyps that close into the red skeleton when touched.  
Colour: Blood red stems, white polyps. Only the lamp shows the red at this depth.  
Scale: Hand to forearm high; "inextricable plots" under rock.

## Bubblegum coral (Paragorgia arborea): The coral kingdom
> an immense forest of large mineral vegetations, enormous petrified trees (at about 300 yards)  
Shape: A true tree shape: thick trunk, heavy branches ending in round knobs. The only coral that makes "trees" deep down.  
Colour: Pink-red or white; under the lamp only.  
Scale: Up to several metres tall and wide: the cemetery forest.

## A real submarine eruption (West Mata volcano, 1,200 m): The volcano
> a large crater was vomiting forth torrents of lava ... no flames ... turn it to vapour  
Shape: Not a glowing cone. Black rubble and broken rock; lava glows red only in cracks and splashes; billowing pale vapour and gas lit orange from below; black stones thrown out and falling (Verne's "rain of stones and scoriae").  
Colour: Red-orange glow in the cracks, pale grey-white steam lit warm; everything else black.  
Scale: The vent is tens of metres across; the cone is a heap of rubble, not a smooth peak.
- a_real_submarine_eruption_0.jpg: NOAA Ocean Exploration · public domain · West Mata submarine volcano erupting, 1,200 m, Lau Basin (2009) (https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/)
- a_real_submarine_eruption_1.jpg: NOAA Ocean Exploration · public domain · Fresh lava on the sea floor (https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/)

## Pillow lava (Basalt erupted under water): Rock and floor
> vast heaps of stone ... rocks, the cromlechs  
Shape: Rounded "pillows" and tubes of black rock heaped on each other, cracked rinds; sediment settled in the hollows.  
Colour: Black and grey, a pale skin of sediment on top.  
Scale: Each pillow a metre across: the ground of the volcano's slopes.
